This is probably too difficult of a paper for the students. But it could work as one kind of capstone paper, showing the application of many of the ideas/frameworks/distinctions we have seen.

To use it that way, ideally, the students would have read Grice (1989), Donnellan (1966), Frege (1948), Lewis (1979), Austin (1962), something by Bettcher on the meaning of ‘woman’, something by wittgenstein on private language, something on demonstratives, maybe something on homophonic names.

Students reading this will need to know about speech acts and propositions.

  • Law is made or unmade via speech acts.
  • The law is a set of true propositions of the form, ‘According to the law of J, P’.
  • Legal effect of a speech act modeled as a pair of sets: the propositions added and the propositions subtracted.

Cites Grice 1989 on meaning.

if textualism or intentionalism or any other determinate view of these matters is correct as an account of any part of the law—Bolivian criminal law, for example, or US constitutional lawthis will be thanks to special features of the relevant field of law and to contingent features of the legal regime in question. To a significant degree, the true principles connecting the linguistic features of legal utterances to their legal effects are a matter of positive legal fact, grounded in the legal history and practice of the community. The nature of law does not settle which of the many possible rules is in fact in place in the same sense in which it does not settle the details of the traffic code.

The talk of “changing the legal score” on p. 134: would be helpful for folks to have read Lewis (1979)

the point about demonstratives and domain restrictions on p. 139 requires some background in those topics.

the point on p. 144 about homophonic proper names is a nice echo of the issue from Frege et al.

the case of Soper’s estate pairs with Donnellan (1966) (who is explicitly cited, along with Kripke (1977), on p. 150)

The material on “personal meaning” would pair well with Wittgenstein on private language.
