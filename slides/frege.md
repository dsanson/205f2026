# Review

## Proper Names, Senses, Referents

- A proper name is a word or phrase that designates a single determinate thing.
- Each proper name **expresses** a sense and **refers to** a referent.
- The relation between name and sense is conventional; the relation between sense and referent is not.

## Senses

- A sense is a "mode of presentation" or a "way of thinking about" the referent.
- Senses are not subjective "conceptions", but shared objective thoughts:
  otherwise, communication would be impossible
- "One can hardly deny that mankind has a common store of thoughts which is
  transmitted from one generation to another" p. 212
- To know what a word means is to grasp the sense it expresses.

## A Note about 'Thought'

- The word thought is subject to an "act-object" ambiguity.
- Examples: 'work', 'belief', 'perception'
- In each case, there is, on the one hand, the process or act or state---the
  work, the belief, the perception---and, on the other hand, the product or
  content---the work, the belief, the perception.
- Frege is saying that we have access to a shared store of thought *contents*.
  He is not saying that we are part of a hive mind that engages in collective
  acts of thought.

## Names express Senses

- The relation between a name and the sense it expresses is conventional and arbitrary.
- Two names can express the same sense: **synonyms**, but also **translation**.
- One name should not be used to express more than one sense: **ambiguity**, which should be avoided.
- No name should fail to express a sense: **nonsense**, which should be avoided.

## Senses and Referents

- The sense of a proper name determines a single unique referent---or no referent at all.
- The relation between a sense and the referent it determines is not conventional.
- The same referent can be determined by many different senses---there can be many different ways of thinking about the same thing.
- Some proper names express senses that fail to determine any referent at all: **empty names**.

## Compositionality

- The meaning of a whole sentence is a function of the meanings of its parts.
- Compositionality explains your ability to understand sentences you have never heard before:
- 'The purple twinkies that you left in the drawer spontaneously combusted.'

## Compositionality of Sense and Reference

- The sense expressed by a whole sentence---the thought it expresses---is a
  function of the senses expressed by its parts.
- The referent of a whole sentence---its truth value---is a function of the
  referents of its parts.

## Compositionality and Empty Names

- If a sentence contains a proper name that fails to express any sense---a nonsense word---that sentence fails to express a thought.
- If a sentence contains a proper name that expresses a sense, but has no referent, that sentence expresses a thought, but has no truth value.

## Compositionality and Substitutivity

- The referent---truth value---of a sentence is a function of the referents of its
  parts.
- So, if you replace a proper name with another proper name that has the same
  referent---even if it has a different sense---this should not affect the truth
  value.

## Idealism and Skepticism

-  **Idealism** is the view that there is no external world beyond our ideas.
-  **Skepticism** is the view that, for all we know, there is no external world beyond our ideas.
-  Both might seem like good reasons to hold, with Locke, that words can only be signs for ideas, not objects in reality.

Re-read p. 214. What is Frege's take on these issues?
